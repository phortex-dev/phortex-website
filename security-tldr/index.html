#1Patch nowHot

Freezer attacks spread to France's national blood service

Attackers changed freezer setpoints from about -30°C to 110°C at a contractor serving France's blood service overnight on November 1-2, affecting plasma for transfusions. Staff caught it in time; how they got in is not yet known.

Do this: Ask whoever services your fridges, HVAC or alarms how they connect remotely, require a VPN and unique passwords, and turn on temperature alerts that go to your phone.

#2MalwareHot

Hijacked cPGuard update planted a backdoor on Linux web servers

An attacker used stolen credentials to push a malicious cPGuard update on November 2 that installed a backdoor and copied password hashes, SSH keys and database settings. Servers that installed versions 5.88.12 to 5.88.19 are affected.

Do this: If your host or IT team runs cPGuard, update to 5.88.25 or later, check the install log for 5.88.12-5.88.19, and if found reset root and database passwords and replace SSH keys.

A command injection bug in Dameware 2026.3 and earlier lets any logged-in user, even a low-privilege one, take over the computers it manages. SolarWinds released 2026.3.1 on November 3; no attacks are reported yet.

Do this: Upgrade every Dameware install to 2026.3.1, limit access to it to your internal network or VPN, and remove Dameware accounts people no longer need.

#1Patch nowHot

Second wave of hacks turns up supermarket freezers across Europe

Attackers reached internet-exposed refrigeration controllers on November 2 and set freezers to about 50°C in more than 50 Belgian stores plus shops in five other countries, spoiling stock. Many units were older models that no longer get updates.

Do this: List building controllers you own (HVAC, fridges, alarms, cameras), take any reachable from the internet offline or behind a VPN, and replace unsupported units.
