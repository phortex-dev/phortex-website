The six steps

0/6 done
STEP 1

Take inventory

THIS MONTH

You can’t migrate what you can’t see. List every system that encrypts or signs anything.

  • Start from the map above and add your own systems.
  • Record the algorithm and key size where you can, and who controls each one.
  • A scanner or the PQC Toolkit speeds this up for anything internet-facing.
STEP 2

Rank by data shelf life

THIS MONTH

Harvest-now attacks only matter for data that must stay secret for years. Fix those paths first.

  • Mark where patient, client, financial, legal or identity data travels.
  • Anything that must stay private past 2035 goes to the top.
  • Public or short-lived data can wait for normal upgrades.
STEP 3

Ask your vendors

THIS QUARTER

Most of your encryption is run by vendors. Their roadmap is your roadmap.

  • Send the six questions below to each key vendor.
  • Note who has a date, who is vague, and who has no plan.
  • Flag no-plan vendors for review at renewal.
STEP 4

Turn on what already exists

THIS QUARTER

Hybrid post-quantum TLS is already available in many CDNs, load balancers and current server software.

  • Enable hybrid key exchange (X25519MLKEM768) for your website where your host or CDN offers it.
  • Keep browsers, operating systems and OpenSSL-based servers current (OpenSSL 3.5 or later).
  • Test after enabling: older firewalls can choke on the larger handshakes.
STEP 5

Build crypto agility

ONGOING

NIST may add or change algorithms again. Systems that can switch by configuration save you a second migration.

  • Prefer settings over hard-coded algorithms in anything you build or commission.
  • Automate certificate renewal so swapping certificate types is routine.
  • Keep your inventory current; re-check it every six months.
STEP 6

Plan replacements to 2030

NEXT BUDGET CYCLE

Some hardware will never get post-quantum support. Replacing it on schedule costs far less than in a rush.

  • Make PQC support (FIPS 203) a written requirement for every new purchase.
  • Budget VPN, firewall and device replacements on their normal cycles before 2030.
  • Document the plan; customers and insurers are starting to ask.

Six questions for every vendor

Send these to your VPN, firewall, hosting, identity, payment and line-of-business software providers. Vague answers are an answer.

  1. Which post-quantum algorithms does your product support today, by FIPS number? When will ML-KEM (FIPS 203) be on by default?
  2. Do you support hybrid key exchange, such as X25519MLKEM768?
  3. Can algorithms be changed with a software update, or will this need new hardware?
  4. When will your FIPS 140-3 validated modules cover the new algorithms?
  5. Can you provide a cryptographic bill of materials (CBOM) for your product?
  6. What is your plan and date for retiring RSA and elliptic-curve cryptography?
