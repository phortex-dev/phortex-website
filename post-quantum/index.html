Straight answers

Is this hype? Quantum computers can’t break anything yet.

True today. But the estimated machine size needed to break RSA-2048 keeps shrinking, from about 20 million noisy qubits (2019) to under a million (2025), and the US government has set firm migration dates anyway. Data you send now can be stored and broken later.

We’re not a federal agency. Does this apply to us?

Indirectly, yes. Federal contractors face PQC requirements by 2030, and they will push them down to their suppliers. NIST’s deprecation dates also steer what vendors, browsers and auditors support.

Won’t our vendors just handle it?

Many will, for cloud services. Older VPN appliances, firewalls, line-of-business software and devices you own often won’t, and you need to know which is which before budgets are set.

Do we need to replace AES encryption too?

Generally no. Quantum attacks hit public-key cryptography (RSA, elliptic curves) hardest. Symmetric encryption like AES-256 remains strong at that key size.
