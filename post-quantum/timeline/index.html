The timeline: every date that matters

The deadlines are set by policy, not by when a quantum computer shows up. NIST notes that past cryptographic transitions took 10 to 20 years, and the federal government now wants its most important systems done by 2030.

From competition to cutoff

  1. Dec 2016EVERYONE
    NIST opens a worldwide competition

    Cryptographers submit candidate algorithms for public attack and review.

  2. Jul 2022EVERYONE
    First four algorithms selected

    Kyber, Dilithium, SPHINCS+ and FALCON advance to standardization.

  3. Aug 2024EVERYONE
    FIPS 203, 204 and 205 published

    ML-KEM, ML-DSA and SLH-DSA become official standards. Vendors can now build to them.

  4. Mar 2025EVERYONE
    HQC chosen as a backup

    A second key-exchange algorithm on different math, in case lattices are ever weakened.

  5. Jun 22, 2026FEDERAL & CONTRACTORS
    Executive Order 14412 signed

    Sets hard federal deadlines and directs a contractor rule and cryptographic bill-of-materials guidance.

  6. Oct 27, 2026EVERYONE
    NIST IR 8547 finalized

    The official roadmap for retiring quantum-vulnerable algorithms.

  7. YOU ARE HERE · NOVEMBER 2026
  8. ~Dec 2026FEDERAL & CONTRACTORS
    Proposed contractor rule due

    The FAR Council is directed to propose requiring covered federal contractors to meet NIST FIPS standards, including PQC.

  9. ~Mar 2027EVERYONE
    Cryptographic bill-of-materials guidance

    CISA and NIST to publish minimum elements for a CBOM: an inventory of the cryptography inside products.

  10. Dec 31, 2030FEDERAL & CONTRACTORS
    Federal key-exchange deadline

    High-value federal systems must use PQC key exchange; covered contractors must comply with the FIPS rule.

  11. After 2030EVERYONE
    RSA and ECC deprecated

    NIST deprecates quantum-vulnerable algorithms. Expect vendors and auditors to start flagging them.

  12. Dec 31, 2031FEDERAL & CONTRACTORS
    Federal signature deadline

    High-value federal systems must use PQC digital signatures.

  13. 2035EVERYONE
    RSA and ECC disallowed

    NIST disallows quantum-vulnerable algorithms; the national goal for completing federal migration.

Vendors, browsers, cloud providers and cyber insurers follow NIST’s dates too, so the change arrives whether or not a contract requires it.

The takeaway

Hardware and software you buy in 2027 will likely still be running in 2031. Make post-quantum support a buying requirement now, and you avoid paying for the same replacement twice.
